# Project understanding milestones

The scientific question is how known interventions change the number of queries needed to simulate noise generated by an unknown reflection. The project connects a channel's capacity to remove entropy, a stability theorem for an entire controlled evolution, and a sharp simulation-query law on a two-qubit target.

This map contains exactly **30 milestones**, organized around that scientific connection. The single educational anchor is John Watrous, **The Theory of Quantum Information** (2018), available through the [author's book page][watrous]. The [Watrous bridge](WATROUS_BRIDGE.md) identifies the relevant foundations and the additional derivations supplied by this project. Research papers linked below supply attribution or imported proof ingredients; they are not additional educational anchors.

The map identifies central understanding targets and their logical dependencies. Complete proofs, technical details, and exact resource conventions remain in the linked [scientific account](../science/README.md). Project entropy formulas use natural logarithms; the book's entropy in bits is converted by multiplying by $`\ln2`$.

## Allocation and route

There are **10 background/preliminaries milestones** and **20 narrative/innovation milestones**. The background builds from finite-dimensional states and operations to the complete target, oracle, and error contract. Ten milestones suffice because the project uses a focused part of finite-dimensional quantum information.

The larger narrative allocation reflects three linked arguments with distinct conceptual work: the entropy/variance converse, the exact mixed-reset construction, and the matching operational query upper and lower bounds. The final milestones connect these arguments to retained memory, physical rate normalization, attribution, and the precise contribution. The allocation follows these scientific dependencies rather than the structure of the textbook.

| Phase and range | Scientific role | Integrating endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Background/preliminaries, [M01](#m01)–[M10](#m10) | Assemble states, channels, distances, entropy, reflection noise, and ordinary queries into one model. | M10 specifies the complete controlled-simulation task. |
| Narrative/innovation, [M11](#m11)–[M17](#m17) | Join control capacity, entropy production, and backward variance into a universal channel bound. | M17 states and interprets the general diamond stability theorem. |
| Narrative/innovation, [M18](#m18)–[M21](#m21) | Turn one unknown reflection and adjustable reset purity into an exact finite-distance witness. | M21 covers every allowed duration and capacity allowance. |
| Narrative/innovation, [M22](#m22)–[M25](#m25) | Combine bounded trajectory sampling, anchored learning, and oracle hybrids. | M25 obtains the matching fixed-dimensional query law. |
| Narrative/innovation, [M26](#m26)–[M30](#m30) | Relate the main result to local noise, memory, interventions, attribution, and output conventions. | M30 integrates the contribution and its operational boundaries. |

Each milestone's dependency list refers only to earlier milestones. Source pointers can refer to later parts of the scientific account because they identify the authoritative result being understood.

## Phase I — Background/preliminaries

<a id="m01"></a>
### M01 — States, observables, and reflections

**Central question.** What does a finite matrix describe in this problem, and what is special about a reflection?

**Understanding target.** Explain a density matrix as a positive, trace-one description of a state; read $`\mathrm{Tr}(\rho O)`$ as an observable expectation; and distinguish state mixtures from coherent superpositions. Recognize that a Hermitian unitary $`U`$ has eigenvalues $`\pm1`$ and satisfies $`U^2=I`$. Interpret the operator norm as the largest output norm from a unit input, and $`\|U-V\|_{\mathrm{op}}`$ as the largest difference between the two actions on such an input.

**Dependencies and story role.** No earlier milestone. This supplies the local objects from which the noise operator, state evolution, and measurable output will be built.

**Conceptual connection.** The same reflection is both an oracle that acts on vectors and a jump operator that acts on density matrices by conjugation. The involution identity later permits exact parity reduction.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§1.1.3, decompositions and norms][w1], and [§2.1.2, quantum states][w2]. Project: [state and norm conventions](../science/model.md#channels-entropy-and-distance) and [the reflection target](../science/model.md#a-controlled-reflection-target).

<a id="m02"></a>
### M02 — Composite systems and retained records

**Central question.** Which registers belong to the evolving system, and how can correlations matter even when a reference is never touched?

**Understanding target.** Explain tensor products, partial traces, marginal states, and an entangled input with an untouched reference. Identify the full retained dimension $`D`$, including quantum memory and records generated during sensing that remain available at final readout. A record produced by the protocol is not an untouched reference merely because later controls ignore it.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01. It joins local states into the complete physical input and output, making subsequent dimension and entropy accounting meaningful.

**Conceptual connection.** The reference expands the class of input correlations against which a simulator must succeed. Retained records instead expand the evolving system whose finite entropy room constrains the protocol. A final measurement applied after the completed target can be handled by data processing.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§2.1.3, reduced states and purifications][w2]. Project: [Theorem S and its retained-register convention](../science/model.md#theorem-s-hermitian-noise-stability-with-controls) and [capacity, reset count, and retained memory](../science/interpretation.md#capacity-reset-count-and-retained-memory).

<a id="m03"></a>
### M03 — Channels and discarded environments

**Central question.** How can an evolution that includes randomness or discarding still be one well-defined physical map?

**Understanding target.** Explain complete positivity and trace preservation, and relate Kraus and Stinespring representations to an unconditioned output. Recognize a mixture of unitary operations, a subsystem replacement, and a measurement with its outcome discarded as channels. Distinguish retaining an environment or outcome from tracing it out.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01–M02. It extends reversible operations to the physical objects used for both noisy intervals and known controls.

**Conceptual connection.** The target must be reproduced as a CPTP channel on arbitrary input. Sampling a branch and averaging its output is a valid implementation; selecting a favorable branch changes the requested output model.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2, channel definitions and representations][w2]. Project: [channel conventions](../science/model.md#channels-entropy-and-distance), [discarded-environment capacity](../science/entropy_capacity.md#5-an-implementation-bound-from-a-discarded-environment), and [the exact reset cycle](../science/mixed_resets.md#2-exact-cycle-and-output).

<a id="m04"></a>
### M04 — Forward states and backward observables

**Central question.** How can the same experiment be described by evolving its state forward or its final observable backward?

**Understanding target.** Use the adjoint relation

```math
\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi(\rho)O)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\Phi^\dagger(O)).
```

Explain why a trace-preserving channel has a unital adjoint and why an observable propagated through all future operations describes the eventual readout at an earlier time. Keep the composition order of the controls consistent in the two descriptions.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01–M03. It supplies the paired descriptions that will make the perturbation and variance arguments fit together.

**Conceptual connection.** The proof will bound two effects of a changed jump: one involving the evolving state and one involving the eventual observable. The latter is controlled by complete positivity of the backward map even when a forward control removes entropy.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2, including Proposition 2.18 and its following remark, and Theorem 2.26][w2]. Project: [interpolation and paired propagation](../science/channel_stability.md#4-interpolation-and-the-entropy-budget) and [the backward variance budget](../science/channel_stability.md#5-a-matched-variance-budget-survives-cptp-controls).

<a id="m05"></a>
### M05 — Trace distance and the full channel task

**Central question.** What does distinguishing two output states establish, and what stronger guarantee does diamond approximation require?

**Understanding target.** Relate the full trace norm $`\|\rho-\sigma\|_1`$ to equal-prior discrimination success $`1/2+\|\rho-\sigma\|_1/4`$. Explain that one chosen input can witness a channel lower bound, while a diamond guarantee controls every input, including one entangled with an untouched reference. Recognize why a reference of input dimension suffices.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01–M04. It gives an operational error criterion for the composite channel framework.

**Conceptual connection.** The two-qubit example will prove a finite output gap on one known probe. A simulator required to approximate the full target must preserve that gap, while the stability upper must work uniformly over all probes and references.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§3.1.1, binary state discrimination][w3], and [§§3.3.1–3.3.3, including Theorems 3.51–3.52][w3]. Project: [distance conventions](../science/model.md#channels-entropy-and-distance) and [the full diamond reduction](../science/channel_stability.md#why-optimization-over-states-gives-the-full-diamond-norm).

<a id="m06"></a>
### M06 — Entropy and conditional-entropy room

**Central question.** How much entropy can a finite retained system gain, including when it is correlated with a reference?

**Understanding target.** Explain von Neumann entropy, the maximum $`\ln D`$, mutual information, and conditional entropy. Understand the range

```math
-\ln D\le S(A|R)\le\ln D
```

for a retained system $`A`$ of dimension $`D`$. Negative conditional entropy describes quantum correlations; it does not mean that a density matrix has negative entropy.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01–M03. It adds a finite information budget to the physical system and reference identified earlier.

**Conceptual connection.** The untouched reference's entropy stays fixed. Consequently the entropy increase available to noise is bounded by a conditional-entropy range determined by the retained system, rather than by an arbitrarily large reference dimension.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§5.2.1–5.2.2, including Theorems 5.24–5.25][w5]. Project: [the reference accounting](../science/entropy_capacity.md#reference-invariance), [the entropy budget](../science/channel_stability.md#4-interpolation-and-the-entropy-budget), and [the state-dependent refinement](../science/channel_stability.md#a-specified-initial-state).

<a id="m07"></a>
### M07 — Relative entropy, data processing, and Pinsker

**Central question.** How can an entropy comparison bound the distinguishability of two nearby states?

**Understanding target.** Interpret relative entropy as an asymmetric state comparison and explain its contraction under a common channel. Relate this to contraction of mutual information under a local operation. With natural logarithms, use quantum Pinsker in the form

```math
D(\rho\|\sigma)\ge\frac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1^2.
```

Keep the logarithm conversion separate from the choice of full trace norm.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M03, M05, and M06. It connects entropy accounting to the operational distance already chosen.

**Conceptual connection.** Data processing will establish reference-safe control capacity. Pinsker applied to a short unitary orbit will turn noise-generated entropy into a quantitative state-commutator bound.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§5.2.3, Theorem 5.35 and Theorem 5.38][w5]. Project: [the natural-log Pinsker proof](../science/channel_stability.md#21-quantum-pinsker-with-natural-logarithms) and [the documented convention conversion](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#watrous2018).

<a id="m08"></a>
### M08 — Reflection noise and the Poisson clock

**Central question.** How does repeated random application of a reflection generate continuous-time dephasing?

**Understanding target.** Read $`\mathcal L_U=\mathrm{Ad}_U-\mathrm{Id}`$ as a rate-one random-jump generator and explain the Poisson count of jumps over a duration. Use $`U^2=I`$ to reduce an uninterrupted interval to an even/odd mixture. Distinguish total jump rate one from the coherence-decay rate two and, for several local jumps, from the rate experienced by each site.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01 and M03. It supplies the concrete noise model rather than assuming a general open-system theory.

**Conceptual connection.** An uninterrupted reflection interval has a one-query implementation. Intervening controls can obstruct combining all intervals into one global parity, making the placement of those controls part of the algorithmic problem.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2, mixtures and representations of channels][w2]. Project derivation: [the reflection target](../science/model.md#a-controlled-reflection-target), [uninterrupted parity](../science/interpretation.md#one-reflection-without-interventions), and [local exposure normalization](../science/memory_and_local_noise.md#1-normalize-the-physical-exposure).

<a id="m09"></a>
### M09 — Controlled oracles, phase, and query cost

**Central question.** What information and resources are supplied by one ordinary controlled-reflection query?

**Understanding target.** Explain the oracle $`\mathrm{c}U=\mathrm{diag}(I,U)`$, the cost of each application, and the use of separate learning probes. Distinguish the physically irrelevant global phase of an isolated unitary channel from a relative phase anchored by a known controlled identity block. Separate unknown-oracle calls from known gates, classical computation, and simulator workspace.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01, M03, and M05. It connects the physical unknown operation to the computational access model.

**Conceptual connection.** The oracle enables a phase-consistent reflection estimate, while ordinary-query distinguishability limits every simulator, including adaptive algorithms with large auxiliary memory. Known integer powers of a reflection reduce to $`I`$ or $`U`$ by parity; interleaved target controls can still prevent reducing the complete evolution to a single parity sample.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§3.3.3, distances between channels][w3], for the unitary-channel distance foundation. Project: [oracle access and the simulator](../science/model.md#oracle-access-and-the-simulator) and [the anchored-unitary construction](../science/query_complexity.md#31-learn-an-anchored-unitary).

<a id="m10"></a>
### M10 — The complete controlled-simulation framework

**Central question.** What precisely must be held fixed, supplied, charged, and reproduced before a simulation claim has a definite meaning?

**Understanding target.** Assemble the target descriptor

```math
\Phi_U=C_r e^{t_r\mathcal L_U}C_{r-1}\cdots C_1e^{t_1\mathcal L_U}C_0,
\qquad T=\sum_k t_k.
```

Identify the unknown reflection, known oracle-independent CPTP controls, finite retained dimension, total noisy duration, controlled-query access, and full unconditioned diamond-error requirement. Define the supplied control promise by $`\kappa(C)=\max_\rho[S(\rho)-S(C\rho)]`$ and $`E_{\mathrm{used}}=\sum_k\kappa(C_k)\le E`$. Initial and terminal controls belong to the descriptor. The principal query problem fixes $`D=4`$ and error $`0.01`$, with real $`T\ge1`$ and allowance $`E\ge0`$. The target's allowance does not constrain the simulator's own probe preparation or resets.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01–M09. This closes the background phase by integrating its objects into one operational problem.

**Conceptual connection.** The scientific claim is a worst-case query classification over allowed target descriptors. Understanding the fixed input/output and access contract makes it possible to compare a universal stability bound, a particular physical witness, and an arbitrary simulation algorithm.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2][w2] and [§§3.3.1–3.3.3][w3] supply the channel and discrimination framework. Project: [the complete model](../science/model.md) and [the exact query contract](../science/query_complexity.md#query-theorem). The control allowance is developed next in M11–M12.

## Phase II — Narrative/innovation

<a id="m11"></a>
### M11 — Entropy-decrease capacity and unital controls

**Central question.** Which property of a known control quantifies its ability to make room for further noise-generated entropy?

**Understanding target.** Explain

```math
\kappa(C)=\max_\rho\{S(\rho)-S(C\rho)\}.
```

Understand why this quantity is nonnegative on equal finite input and output dimensions, why it vanishes for every unital channel, and why zero capacity conversely forces unitality. Recognize it as minus the established minimum entropy gain.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M03, M06–M07, and M10. It introduces the local control resource before using it for an entire protocol.

**Conceptual connection.** Unital operations can alter the target channel and its computational difficulty while removing no entropy. Nonunital controls can replenish the entropy room that noise uses, with a cost defined directly from their action on states.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§5.2.2 and Theorem 5.35][w5]. Project: [capacity and elementary bounds](../science/entropy_capacity.md#definition), including [zero capacity and unitality](../science/entropy_capacity.md#zero-capacity-is-equivalent-to-unitality). Attribution: [Alicki's minimum entropy gain](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#alicki2004).

<a id="m12"></a>
### M12 — References, reset purity, and a sequence budget

**Central question.** How does a local entropy capacity become a reliable resource budget for correlated inputs and repeated interventions?

**Understanding target.** Explain why mutual-information data processing prevents an untouched reference from increasing a control's entropy decrease. Relate composition, tensor products, and convex mixtures of controls to their capacities. For replacing an $`a`$-dimensional subsystem by $`\tau`$, obtain $`\kappa=\ln a-S(\tau)`$. Distinguish the supplied allowance $`E`$, the sum $`E_{\mathrm{used}}=\sum_k\kappa(C_k)\le E`$, and the entropy actually removed on a particular evolving state.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M02, M06–M07, M10, and M11. It promotes the local resource to the reference-safe, sequence-level accounting needed by the converse.

**Conceptual connection.** Reset purity controls capacity, while the number of reset operations is a separate resource. Every specified initial or terminal control contributes to the sum; retaining a record and discarding it inside a control require different accounting.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§5.2.2 and Theorem 5.35][w5]. Project: [reference invariance](../science/entropy_capacity.md#reference-invariance), [composition, products, and mixtures](../science/entropy_capacity.md#3-composition-products-and-mixtures), [exact replacement capacity](../science/entropy_capacity.md#reset-capacity), and [the sequence budget](../science/entropy_capacity.md#control-budget).

<a id="m13"></a>
### M13 — Entropy production controls a commutator

**Central question.** How can the entropy produced by Hermitian noise bound a state's noncommutation with its jump operator?

**Understanding target.** Begin with a full-rank state and follow the short unitary curve $`e^{ixH}\rho e^{-ixH}`$: its entropy is constant, its first-order state change is a commutator, and its relative entropy from $`\rho`$ begins at second order. Combining this expansion with Pinsker gives

```math
\|[H,\rho]\|_1^2\le2\sigma_H(\rho),
\qquad
\sigma_H(\rho)=-\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal D_H(\rho)\ln\rho).
```

Here $`[H,\rho]=H\rho-\rho H`$ and $`\mathcal D_H(\rho)=-[H,[H,\rho]]/2`$. Explain why $`\sigma_H`$ is the entropy-production rate of this Hermitian dissipator.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M04, M07–M08, and M12. This supplies the forward-state part of the stability proof.

**Conceptual connection.** A small jump perturbation couples to $`[H,\rho]`$. Entropy production bounds that coupling without a mixing or spectral-gap assumption. A common Hamiltonian changes the state but contributes zero to its entropy derivative.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [Theorem 5.38][w5]. Project derivation: [the unitary-curve lemma](../science/channel_stability.md#unitary-curve) and [the entropy derivative](../science/channel_stability.md#23-entropy-derivative-during-a-noisy-interval).

<a id="m14"></a>
### M14 — The backward-observable variance budget

**Central question.** What limits the perturbation term involving the future readout rather than the current state?

**Understanding target.** Pair a forward state $`\rho_s`$ with a backward Hermitian observable $`O_s`$ of norm at most one. Understand why the noisy evolution increases $`g(s)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_sO_s^2)`$ by a sum of squared observable commutators. At a common CPTP control, the adjoint is unital and completely positive, so its operator Schwarz inequality makes the corresponding jump in $`g`$ nonnegative. The full integrated variance term is therefore at most one.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M03–M04, M08, and M12. It supplies the second, complementary budget before the perturbation estimate combines the two.

**Conceptual connection.** Forward controls may lower entropy, but the backward variance estimate survives because it uses complete positivity and trace preservation. Common Hamiltonian terms cancel through the Leibniz rule.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2][w2] supply the CP-map framework. Project derivation: [the matched variance identity](../science/channel_stability.md#5-a-matched-variance-budget-survives-cptp-controls) and [its control-boundary inequality](../science/channel_stability.md#the-jump-at-a-control-boundary).

<a id="m15"></a>
### M15 — Two perturbation terms and weighted integration

**Central question.** How do the entropy and variance budgets replace a naive linear accumulation of local error?

**Understanding target.** Explain why differentiating a Hermitian dissipator produces two commutator terms. One is controlled by the state commutator from M13; the other is controlled by the observable commutator from M14. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz over jump labels and time with common weights $`p_j(s)\ge0`$ satisfying $`\sum_jp_j(s)=1`$, giving an integrated perturbation scale

```math
V^2=\int_0^T\sum_jp_j(s)\|A_j(s)-B_j(s)\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\,ds.
```

For a constant jump distance, this scale grows as $`\sqrt T`$.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01, M04, and M13–M14. It joins the two local proof mechanisms into the quantitative estimate used for a whole path of channels.

**Conceptual connection.** Squaring and integrating the perturbation is useful because the other two integrals have finite budgets. These paired estimates produce the time and entropy dependence of the bound.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§1.1.3, operator norms][w1]. Project: [both commutator estimates and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz](../science/channel_stability.md#weighted-cauchy-schwarz).

<a id="m16"></a>
### M16 — Interpolation and singular control boundaries

**Central question.** How can an estimate along smooth, full-rank evolutions prove a bound for arbitrary endpoints and pure resets?

**Understanding target.** Explain why linear interpolation between matched Hermitian jumps remains a valid Hermitian-noise generator, even when the endpoints are reflections and intermediate jumps are not. Apply Duhamel differentiation through the same finite control sequence. Understand why regularizing only the initial state fails after a pure reset, and why mixing every control with complete depolarization restores full rank without increasing its capacity budget. Take the finite-composition limit back to the target channel.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M03, M10–M12, and M15. It turns the local perturbation estimate into a rigorous endpoint comparison covering the interventions used by the construction.

**Conceptual connection.** Positivity, entropy logarithms, and common-control placement must all be preserved by the proof's auxiliary path. The regularization is a proof device; it does not alter the final target or charge extra entropy capacity.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2][w2] and [§5.2.2, including Theorem 5.26][w5]. Project: [interpolation and entropy telescoping](../science/channel_stability.md#4-interpolation-and-the-entropy-budget) and [singular inputs and pure resets](../science/channel_stability.md#singular-states).

<a id="m17"></a>
### M17 — The general diamond stability theorem

**Central question.** What universal statement follows when the two budgets and endpoint argument are combined?

**Understanding target.** Assemble

```math
\|\Phi_A-\Phi_B\|_\diamond
\le V\left[1+\sqrt{4\ln D+2E}\right].
```

Explain the $`2\ln D+E`$ entropy budget, the unit variance budget, and the optimization over all inputs and untouched references. State the scope: bounded, piecewise continuous matched Hermitian jumps and common Hamiltonian, common weights, and finitely many common known CPTP controls. Relate the result to the trivial distance cap and the elementary linear-time cap for bounded endpoints.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M05–M07 and M11–M16. This integrates the full converse before introducing its saturating example.

**Conceptual connection.** Retained dimension and control entropy capacity limit sensitivity without a gap or mixing assumption. The distance compares the supplied jump representations. It does not by itself provide an efficient high-dimensional channel-synthesis algorithm.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§3.3.3, Theorem 3.51][w3], and [§5.2.2][w5]. Project: [the main stability theorem](../science/channel_stability.md#main-theorem), [its diamond conclusion](../science/channel_stability.md#6-finish-the-channel-bound), and [state-dependent and elementary bounds](../science/channel_stability.md#8-state-dependent-form-and-elementary-caps).

<a id="m18"></a>
### M18 — A syndrome and a logical qubit

**Central question.** How can one unknown reflection on two qubits convert a change of noise axis into a logical signal?

**Understanding target.** Interpret the syndrome qubit $`S`$, logical qubit $`L`$, and reflection

```math
U_\theta=\cos\theta\,X_S-\sin\theta\,Y_SZ_L.
```

Explain how a fixed CNOT relates this encoded operation to local single-qubit axis noise with one noiseless ancillary qubit. Understand the conditional logical rotations associated with a syndrome flip and the oracle-distance bound $`\|U_\theta-U_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le|\theta|`$.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01–M03, M08–M10, and M17. It starts the constructive branch from a concrete local physical mechanism.

**Conceptual connection.** The construction will attain the converse at fixed retained dimension and total rate one. Its chosen known probe is a valid witness input for a full-channel lower bound; it is not a free replacement of the simulator's arbitrary target input.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.1.2–2.1.3][w2]. Project: [the local-noise implementation](../science/mixed_resets.md#1-a-local-noise-implementation) and [the two-qubit reflection in the query proof](../science/query_complexity.md#41-one-reflection-on-two-qubits). Mechanism attribution: [Chen et al.](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#chen2024).

<a id="m19"></a>
### M19 — Exact mixed-reset coherence and entropy use

**Central question.** What does one noisy interval followed by a partially polarized syndrome replacement do exactly?

**Understanding target.** For $`\tau_b=(I+bZ)/2`$ and odd-jump probability $`p=(1-e^{-2\Delta})/2`$, derive the logical coherence multiplier

```math
f_b(\theta)=1-p+p\cos(2\theta)-ipb\sin(2\theta).
```

Repeated cycles multiply coherence by $`f_b(\theta)^N`$. Relate the phase scale $`Nb\theta`$, the visibility loss beginning at order $`N\theta^2`$, and the exact summed capacity $`Ne(b)`$, where $`e(b)=\ln2-h((1+b)/2)=\Theta(b^2)`$ uniformly on $`[0,1]`$.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M08, M12, and M18. It joins the physical mechanism to the capacity resource through an exact finite channel formula.

**Conceptual connection.** Weak polarization makes each replacement inexpensive without reducing the number of interventions or the dissipative rate. On the zero-angle baseline, the actual removed entropy is a constant fraction of $`E_{\mathrm{used}}`$ for the chosen interval durations.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2][w2] and [§5.2.1][w5]. Project: [exact cycle and output](../science/mixed_resets.md#2-exact-cycle-and-output) and [capacity and actual use](../science/mixed_resets.md#3-entropy-capacity-and-its-actual-use).

<a id="m20"></a>
### M20 — Finite separation across polarization regimes

**Central question.** How can the exact output retain a measurable gap both when a coherent phase accumulates and when its first derivative vanishes?

**Understanding target.** For interval duration $`\Delta\ge1`$, explain why $`Nb^2\ge1`$ permits a visible phase at $`\theta=1/(2Nb)`$ while retaining constant visibility. For $`b=0`$, explain the constant dephasing signal at $`\theta=1/(2\sqrt N)`$. Relate the exact trace-norm gap $`|f_b(\theta)^N-1|>0.08`$ to logical $`Y`$ or $`X`$ readout and a fixed binary advantage.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M05, M17, and M19. It turns the exact mechanism into a finite operational witness with uniform constants.

**Conceptual connection.** Zero first-order phase does not imply absence of finite distinguishability. The argument controls phase and visibility together and uses one final readout, with no hidden repetition, selected trajectory, or retained outcome history.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§3.1.1][w3]. Project: [uniform finite separation and readout](../science/mixed_resets.md#4-uniform-finite-separation), and [the two polarization estimates](../science/query_complexity.md#5-uniform-finite-separation-in-every-budget-regime).

<a id="m21"></a>
### M21 — Every duration and every capacity allowance

**Central question.** How does a finite-cycle example meet the exact promises for every real $`T\ge1`$ and every allowance $`E\ge0`$?

**Understanding target.** Use $`N=\lfloor T\rfloor`$ and $`\Delta=T/N\in[1,2)`$ without a leftover interval. Choose zero, partial, or full polarization according to the supplied budget, check $`Ne(b)\le E`$, and count all $`N`$ replacements including the terminal one. Explain why exact equality with the allowance is unnecessary.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M10, M12, and M19–M20. It completes the constructive branch at the same quantifiers as the universal statement.

**Conceptual connection.** The inverse separation angle has order $`\min\{T,\sqrt{T(1+E)}\}`$ in every regime, including small real times and allowances below one pure-qubit reset. This yields sharp finite discrimination under the specified capacity constraint.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§3.1.1][w3] supplies the discrimination meaning; the parameter choices are project derivations. Project: [all times and budgets](../science/mixed_resets.md#5-every-real-exposure-time-and-every-supplied-budget) and [the precise polarization choice](../science/query_complexity.md#53-choose-the-polarization-using-the-supplied-budget).

<a id="m22"></a>
### M22 — A bounded Poisson simulation algorithm

**Central question.** How can a random-trajectory simulator have a bounded query count on every execution?

**Understanding target.** Sample independent Poisson counts for the noisy intervals and use their total distribution $`\mathrm{Pois}(T)`$. Explain why capping the total at $`\lceil10T\rceil`$ and replacing the overflow branch by a fixed known channel produces a valid CPTP simulator with a small diamond error. Distinguish a bounded query guarantee from a mean query count.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M03, M05, M08–M10. It starts the algorithmic branch with a concrete linear-time upper bound.

**Conceptual connection.** Trajectory sampling and the overflow choice occur independently of the unknown target input. The number of control boundaries can affect known computational work while the total Poisson tail depends only on $`T`$. No favorable trajectory is selected.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2][w2] and [§3.3.2][w3] supply mixture and channel-distance foundations. Project derivation: [the bounded direct-trajectory upper bound](../science/query_complexity.md#2-a-bounded-direct-trajectory-upper-bound).

<a id="m23"></a>
### M23 — Anchored unitary learning and reflection fitting

**Central question.** How can an estimate defined only up to global phase become a usable estimate of the supplied reflection?

**Understanding target.** Learn $`W=\mathrm{diag}(I_4,U)`$ as an eight-dimensional unitary using the supplied controlled oracle. Explain how the known identity block determines a computable phase alignment from the estimate alone. Hermitianize the aligned lower block and take its spectral sign, obtaining a valid reflection for every estimate. Use the imported guarantee of $`O(d^2/\zeta)`$ queries for expected squared channel error $`\mathbb E\delta^2\le\zeta^2`$, with $`d=8`$, and the fitted reflection's operator-error bound $`6\delta`$.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M01, M05, M09–M10, and M17. It supplies the operational input to the improved simulation upper bound.

**Conceptual connection.** For this upper bound, channel continuity becomes an operational algorithm when the learner returns a usable description. The expected-squared-error guarantee and the all-estimates phase/reflection fit avoid conditioning the simulation on a successful estimate.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§1.1.3][w1] and [§3.3.3][w3]. Project: [learn an anchored unitary](../science/query_complexity.md#31-learn-an-anchored-unitary) and [fit a reflection for every estimate](../science/query_complexity.md#32-fit-a-reflection-for-every-estimate). Imported research result: [Haah–Kothari–O'Donnell–Tang, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 1.6](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#hkot2023).

<a id="m24"></a>
### M24 — Averaging learned channels into a query upper bound

**Central question.** Why does learning the reflection to a particular accuracy suffice to simulate the entire controlled target?

**Understanding target.** Keep the arbitrary target input untouched while learning on auxiliary probes; then implement the known fitted target channel. Apply stability to each fitted reflection and average the resulting CPTP channels. Use the expected squared learning error to bound the average diamond error, giving $`O(\sqrt{T(1+E)})`$ queries at fixed dimension and error.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M03, M05, M10, M17, and M23. It combines the converse's continuity estimate with an actual synthesis procedure.

**Conceptual connection.** The mathematical expectation is the physical average over estimation outcomes. It adds no postselection or confidence logarithm. The query result allows substantial classical computation and known-gate synthesis, and the target's capacity allowance does not charge the simulator's learning resources.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2][w2] and [§3.3.2][w3]. Project: [implement and average the fitted target](../science/query_complexity.md#33-implement-and-average-the-fitted-target). Imported learning guarantee: [Haah–Kothari–O'Donnell–Tang](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#hkot2023).

<a id="m25"></a>
### M25 — Oracle hybrids and the sharp query law

**Central question.** Why must every successful simulator use as many queries, in order, as the better upper bound?

**Understanding target.** Explain why replacing one oracle call changes an algorithm's output by at most $`2\|U_\theta-U_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}`$, and why a $`q`$-query hybrid therefore gives at most $`2q|\theta|`$ even with arbitrary auxiliary memory and measurements. Subtract the two $`0.01`$ simulation errors from the target's gap greater than $`0.08`$. Combine the resulting inverse-angle lower bound with M22 and M24 to obtain

```math
Q_4(T,E,0.01)=\Theta\!\left(\min\{T,\sqrt{T(1+E)}\}\right).
```

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M05, M09–M10, M21–M24. It integrates the physical construction and both algorithms into a matching worst-case classification.

**Conceptual connection.** The lower bound applies to arbitrary ordinary-query algorithms, not only to the exhibited trajectory or recovery circuits. The full-channel guarantee makes the known witness input sufficient to constrain the simulator.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§3.3.3, including Theorem 3.52 and the following network bound][w3]. Project: [the oracle hybrid](../science/query_complexity.md#6-oracle-hybrid-lower-bound) and [the precise query theorem](../science/query_complexity.md#query-theorem).

<a id="m26"></a>
### M26 — Local GHZ probes and dimension dependence

**Central question.** What elementary physical family shows that retained dimension is a real part of the stability bound?

**Understanding target.** For $`n`$ independent commuting one-spin reflections of weights $`1/n`$, distinguish total duration $`t`$ from local exposure $`t/n`$. Explain how a $`Y`$-basis GHZ probe and parity readout give constant separation at $`t=n`$ and angle of order $`1/n`$, while producing entropy $`nh(r)`$ in a $`2^n`$-dimensional target. Relate the comparison with inputs separable across site-and-private-reference blocks to the role of entanglement, and combine exact per-site sampling with the hybrid witness to understand

```math
Q_{\mathrm{local}}(t,n,0.01)=\Theta(\min\{t,n\}),
\qquad t\ge1,\quad n\in\{1,2,\ldots\}.
```

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M02, M05–M06, M08, M17, and M22–M25. It tests the general bound in a separate, transparent many-spin class.

**Conceptual connection.** This supporting result makes the logarithmic dimension dependence necessary and exhibits its entropy storage mechanism. It uses $`n`$ jump types and its own SELECT oracle; it is distinct from the main single-reflection, fixed-four-dimensional classification.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§2.1.3][w2], [§3.3.2][w3], and [§5.2.2][w5]. Project: [normalization](../science/memory_and_local_noise.md#1-normalize-the-physical-exposure), [exact GHZ signal](../science/memory_and_local_noise.md#2-exact-ghz-signal), [entropy and the separable comparison](../science/memory_and_local_noise.md#3-entropy-and-the-separable-probe-comparison), and [the local query law](../science/memory_and_local_noise.md#4-exact-local-class-query-complexity).

### M27 — Retained memory, pure resets, and activity normalization

**Central question.** How do a limited number of retained qubits and pure resets relate to the entropy theorem, and which metric does their example saturate?

**Understanding target.** For integer $`q\ge2`$, integer $`R\ge0`$, and $`T\ge1`$, consider $`q`$ retained qubits, at most $`R`$ pure-qubit replacements, and other controls that are unital. Translate these resources into $`\ln D\le q\ln2`$ and $`E_{\mathrm{used}}\le R\ln2`$. Explain how retained syndrome patterns and between-block resets give a weighted sensitivity scale $`\min\{T,\sqrt{T(q+R)}\}`$. Track the jump rescaling $`H_j=gB_j`$: for long times it reduces actual dissipative activity and changes the perturbation metric.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M02, M12, M17, M19, and M26. It relates two physical resource accounts to the general theorem while preserving the supporting example's normalization.

**Conceptual connection.** The final syndrome is retained rather than erased for free. Mixed-reset capacity and a count of pure resets are different constraints. Weighted Hermitian-jump sharpness at reduced activity does not imply a raw reflection-query lower bound of the same order; the fixed-rate local class instead saturates at order $`n`$ queries.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§2.1.3][w2] and [§5.2.2][w5]. Project: [the long-time limit and weighted metric](../science/memory_and_local_noise.md#5-the-long-time-limit-and-the-weighted-metric), [retained qubits and pure resets](../science/memory_and_local_noise.md#6-retained-qubits-and-a-bounded-count-of-pure-resets), and [retained-record accounting](../science/interpretation.md#capacity-reset-count-and-retained-memory).

### M28 — Why controls change a single-reflection task

**Central question.** How can one reflection require one, square-root, or linear-in-time query cost under three different intervention classes?

**Understanding target.** Compare uninterrupted evolution, which reduces to one parity sample; the worst case with zero-capacity interventions, which has order $`\sqrt T`$ cost; and targets with entropy-removing interventions, whose worst-case cost grows as $`\min\{T,\sqrt{T(1+E)}\}`$. Explain both roles of control: preventing global parity cancellation and, when nonunital, restoring entropy room.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M08, M10–M12, M17, M19–M21, and M25–M27. It synthesizes the example, converse, and algorithmic classification into a physical explanation.

**Conceptual connection.** Zero capacity still permits repeated unital interventions. An allowance of order $`T`$ can support linear query growth while retaining only two qubits. These are worst-case statements for different target classes; a larger permitted budget does not force every particular target to become harder.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§§2.2.1–2.2.2][w2] and [§5.2.2][w5]. **Understanding target.** Separate textbook entropy and channel inequalities, established minimum entropy gain, imported optimal unitary learning, and prior informative-noise correction or weak-polarization mechanisms from the project's combined entropy-controlled channel converse and query classification. <a id="m30"></a>
### M30 — The contribution and its operational boundaries

**Central question.** What complete scientific claim can be made once the model, converse, construction, and algorithms are understood together?

**Understanding target.** Explain the chain from finite entropy room and common-control capacity to channel sensitivity, from sensitivity to required learning accuracy, and from a finite-distance witness to query hardness. State the matching fixed-$`D=4`$, fixed-error, at-most-budget theorem with ordinary controlled-reflection access and arbitrary-input CPTP output. Keep target capacity, retained dimension, and oracle-query count separate. Identify the more general stability theorem and the supporting local/memory examples as related results with their own scope.

**Dependencies and story role.** Requires M10, M17, M21, and M25–M29. This closes the narrative phase by integrating the result, its mechanism, and its evidence into one account.

**Conceptual connection.** The result concerns an unmonitored full channel and finite binary separation. It supplies neither a QFI bound by squaring a trace-distance coefficient nor a heat/work cost without an implementation model. The query classification holds at fixed dimension and error; it does not classify variable-precision cost, high-dimensional learning efficiency, or end-to-end runtime. Generated retained records count in $`D`$, and unrestricted simulator workspace remains allowed and charged only through its oracle calls.

**Source pointers.** Anchor: Watrous [§3.3][w3] and [§5.2][w5] provide the unified information-theoretic language.
